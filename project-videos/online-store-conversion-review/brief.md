# Project Video Brief

Project: Making a Skincare Store Easier to Shop

Video type: Evidence-led shopping review

Target duration: 42 seconds

Primary viewer: Store owner, website helper, or portfolio reviewer

Story mode: Shopper-path audit

One-sentence job: The review followed the public shopping path, found five places where shoppers could lose confidence, and turned them into seven ordered fixes.

Viewer takeaway: The owner can see where shoppers get stuck, which fixes come first, and what to measure after changes.

Plain story:

- Problem: shoppers had to choose premium botanical oils without enough help connecting products to their needs.
- Solution: follow the public shopping path on desktop and mobile, then use screenshots, stock counts, speed checks, and sources to document the friction.
- Result: a report with five findings, seven fixes in order, and clear results to watch after implementation.

Scene arc:

1. Problem: shoppers could lose confidence before choosing a product.
2. Review path: follow the homepage, assessment, product list, and product page.
3. Findings: five problems made choosing and buying harder.
4. Evidence: the same 18 public pages produced 36 successful desktop and mobile captures.
5. Report: seven fixes were ordered and paired with results to watch.
6. Ending beat: five findings and seven ordered fixes, without claiming a sales increase.

Reading-speed constraint:

- Use one headline and one short sentence at most.
- Keep each scene at or below 18 must-read words.
- Let the public report preview carry the proof.
- Keep the final card settled for 3 seconds.

Assets needed:

- Screenshot: the public shopping-review report preview used by the portfolio case study.
- Additional captures: none. Reframe the one public preview with different positions and contained crops.
- Native graphics: shopper-path rail, four finding cards, and count cards for 5, 36, and 7.

Privacy notes:

- Hide: private store dashboards, sales reports, customer information, website-admin access, and any private account data.
- Do not claim: a sales, conversion, health, or product-outcome improvement before changes are implemented and measured.
- Safe to show: the public report preview, published counts, plain finding names, and the ordered-fix framing.
