<CaseSection title="The starting point">

Desarmadero La Torre is an auto-dismantling yard with a large inventory of parts that changes all the time. Customers had to call or visit to find out what was in stock, so staff spent their day answering the same availability questions.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="What I found">

Customers already asked for parts by make, model, and year, so the catalog could be organized around that same question. The staff needed a simple way to keep listings current, not a heavy inventory system they wouldn't use.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="What I built" media>

I built a public catalog where customers searched by make, model, and year, saw photos, and sent an inquiry about a specific part. Staff updated it through a short listing form.

<ImagePlate image={desarmaderoFeature} alt="Desarmadero La Torre searchable inventory interface." />

The owner then came back to run the yard itself: counter sales, cash-desk payments, dismantle orders, and finding vehicles across 301 parking spots. All of that lived on paper, in WhatsApp, in Excel, and in people's heads. I turned one discovery call into a written spec and a clickable prototype that follows each person's job, from the seller to the cash desk to the dismantler. The prototype is still online for anyone to [try](https://desarmadero.pages.dev/) with the password `asado`.

The owner came back for a second project after the catalog. He clicked through the prototype but didn't go ahead with the full build.
