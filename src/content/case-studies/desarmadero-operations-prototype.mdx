<CaseSection title="One discovery call became a PRD, spec, and clickable prototype">
  I turned one discovery call into a PRD, functional spec, clickable operations prototype, and live demo for an auto-dismantling yard.

    A returning client wanted to go beyond a searchable parts catalog and run the auto-dismantling yard itself. Building the prototype before the full product gave the client something concrete to test and made the next scope conversation clearer.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="The yard ran across paper, chat, spreadsheets, and memory">
  The first project for this client was a searchable parts catalog. It helped customers check stock before calling.

    The second idea was bigger: counter sales, cash desk payments, dismantle orders, assignments, and finding vehicles inside a 301-position yard. The existing system lived across paper budget sheets, WhatsApp, Excel, and people remembering where cars were parked.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="I modeled the yard before pricing the full build">
  <DecisionBlock title="Map how a sale becomes a dismantle order.">
    I began with how the yard actually runs. A counter sale can happen before staff physically pull the parts, so the prototype treats the sale as the beginning of the dismantling process.
  </DecisionBlock>

    <DecisionBlock title="Build before quoting the full system.">
      Instead of jumping straight to a quote, I built a working prototype first. A clickable app is often the fastest way to confirm whether I understood the business.
    </DecisionBlock>

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="The prototype follows each role through the yard" media>

<CaseSection title="The prototype made the operating rules visible">
  <DecisionBlock title="Payment releases the dismantle order.">
    A dismantler cannot see the order until the cash desk records a deposit or full payment. That matched the business rule behind the real operation.
  </DecisionBlock>

    <DecisionBlock title="The yard map replaces memory.">
      The 301-position map makes a vehicle searchable by plate, so finding a car doesn't depend on someone remembering where it is parked.
    </DecisionBlock>

    <DecisionBlock title="Each role sees only the actions it needs.">
      The administrator, seller, cash desk, dismantle manager, and dismantler each get the controls for their part of the process instead of sharing one overloaded back office.
