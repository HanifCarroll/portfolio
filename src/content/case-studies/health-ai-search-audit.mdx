<CaseSection title="Summary">
A founder asked how a health-services business could show up when people ask AI tools for help.

I turned that question into a website audit, search-testing plan, finalized evidence files, and private action report. The public case study stays framed as a careful audit, not a finished impact story.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="The situation">
  The useful answer was not "publish more content" or "add AI keywords."

The founder needed to know what AI tools could already find, which public sources seemed to help, what the site made easy or hard to mention, and which fixes were practical enough to bring into a follow-up conversation.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Constraints">
  <DecisionBlock title="Use public evidence only.">
    The audit did not have a private search dashboard, analytics account, website admin, or server access. That made the boundary clear: inspect the public website, public blog, what search engines could see, and live AI answers.
  </DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock title="Do not change the site during diagnosis.">
  Recommendations had to be useful without making live changes first. The output needed to help the
  founder talk with a developer or give clear tasks to an AI website helper.
</DecisionBlock>

  <DecisionBlock title="Keep health-service claims cautious.">
    The public case study avoids private client details and does not claim medical, compliance, ranking, or conversion outcomes that have not happened.
  </DecisionBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Audit direction" media>
  The preview shows the shape of the private report: site checks, test questions, observations, tasks, and update points in one place.

  <ImagePlate image={auditReportPreview} alt="AI search audit report preview with search checks, evidence counts, findings, and action cards." />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="What I checked">
  <DecisionBlock title="Site and blog setup.">
    The main site appeared to be custom-coded, while the blog appeared to be a separate WordPress site. That split matters because AI tools may read the main site and the blog differently.
  </DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock title="Can search engines read it?">
  The site had useful site maps and pages that search engines could read. The main site's
  search-instructions file, `robots.txt`, returned 404 during the audit, which became a concrete fix
  instead of a guessed blocker.
</DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock title="Clear labels for the business and services.">
  Some detail pages had machine-readable labels for FAQs, page type, and breadcrumbs. The homepage,
  city pages, and about page did not clearly label the business and its services, so the fix was to
  make that context explicit.
</DecisionBlock>

  <DecisionBlock title="AI answer testing.">
    The final evidence file has 74 search-result rows: 15 Perplexity answer rows, 8 live web-search rows, 33 rows checked in a browser, and 18 older manual rows kept for history. The final browser retest added 12 rows: 7 ChatGPT Search, 2 Gemini, and 3 Google AI Mode / AI Overview.
  </DecisionBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Final evidence files">
  <DecisionBlock title="Browser checks replaced the open to-check items.">
    Rows checked in a browser now cover every earlier manual follow-up item. The older manual rows remain in the spreadsheet for history, but the browser-checked rows are the current evidence.
  </DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock title="The evidence separated where the company appeared from where it did not.">
  ChatGPT Search still missed broad Spanish Buenos Aires at-home blood-test searches. Gemini still
  missed Spanish Medellin sexual-health searches. Google and ChatGPT now mentioned the company for
  several Medellin and brand-name searches.
</DecisionBlock>

  <DecisionBlock title="The result is a reusable audit system, not a claimed increase in AI mentions.">
    The evidence files give the founder and whoever helps with the site a test-question list, results spreadsheet, source notes, action-card notes, and handoff notes. It does not claim that the recommendations have been implemented or that AI mentions improved after implementation.
  </DecisionBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Private delivery">
  <DecisionBlock title="The report became a private report site.">
    The audit project now builds pages for the overview, first fixes, next improvements, results, monthly testing, and appendix sections. The private site uses the evidence files and shows 9 action cards without putting the private report on the public portfolio.
  </DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock title="Access is private and search engines are blocked.">
  The report site requires a password before showing the content and tells search engines not to
  list it. The public case study does not include the protected URL, password, or client-sensitive
  report details.
</DecisionBlock>

  <DecisionBlock title="Verification proves delivery, not client impact.">
    The private report builds and checks locally, and the live private site blocks public access as expected. That verifies delivery, not implementation by the client or a measured increase in AI mentions.
  </DecisionBlock>
</CaseSection>
