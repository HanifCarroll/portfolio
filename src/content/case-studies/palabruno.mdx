<CaseSection title="An early prototype needed a first paid version">
Palabruno began as a prototype for a Spanish reader. Learners could tap a word for translation, grammar, and pronunciation help without leaving the text. The founder needed help deciding what belonged in a paid product and taking it through launch.

As a full-stack engineering contractor, I worked directly with the founder on scope, learner and teacher workflows, AI reader development, payments, and release handoff. The engagement took the product to iOS, Android, and web.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Choosing a buyer shaped the workflows">
Tutors already create lesson material and send it to students. I treated them as the first buyer to test and scoped a web workspace around readings, students, groups, and sharing. This was a product hypothesis the founder could test after launch.

Students needed a mobile reader centered on reading, saved texts, and help in context. Teachers needed to prepare and distribute lessons. I kept those interfaces separate while connecting them through the shared backend.

The MVP also needed real payments and store readiness. Late feedback affected UX, pricing, legal copy, performance, and screenshots, so launch work was part of the engagement.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Frontend architecture for learners and teachers">
The product separates a React web workspace from React Native / Expo learner apps. The teacher workspace covers reading creation, student and group management, and sharing; the learner interfaces keep vocabulary and phrase explanations inside the reading flow.

The current web architecture uses TanStack Router and a shared TanStack Query client. Signed-in viewer and access state live at the app route boundary, while TanStack Table handles pagination and selection for substantial dashboard and admin tables. Data fetching and cache updates stay separate from each table’s presentation state.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Building the Spanish reader compiler">
The reader had to connect each tap to the word or phrase being explained. A fluent learning card could still be wrong for the selected text.

I built a reader compiler combining natural language processing (NLP), linguistic rules, and constrained LLM generation to produce tappable words, phrases, and contextual learning cards. Language analysis and structural rules worked alongside model-generated explanations.

Targeted repair handled invalid output, and regression evaluations checked later changes.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Making text processing recoverable">
I implemented durable text-processing workflows with persisted progress and failure recovery. A long-running reading job needed a recoverable state beyond the browser request that started it.

Per-stage latency and model-cost tracking made it possible to inspect where processing time and spend accumulated. That supported diagnosis at the stage that needed attention, alongside the reader’s regression checks.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Integrating payments, launching, and handing off">
  <DecisionBlock title="Put each payment path where its customer used the product.">
    RevenueCat handles learner Premium subscriptions through Apple and Google. Stripe handles Teacher payments on the web, where tutors prepare lessons. Supabase supports the shared backend.
  </DecisionBlock>

  <DecisionBlock title="Leave the founder with release steps and product documentation.">
    I prepared the public site, pricing and legal pages, store listings, screenshots, release documentation, and handoff notes. The founder could operate the product and continue testing the tutor offer after the engagement.
  </DecisionBlock>
</CaseSection>
