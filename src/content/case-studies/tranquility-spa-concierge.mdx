<CaseSection title="The hard part wasn't booking. It was choosing.">
  I built Tranquility as an independent prototype for a fictional spa after an operations consultant described a familiar customer problem: a visitor reaches a long service menu, can't tell which treatment fits, and leaves the decision for later.

The idea went beyond adding a chatbot. The guest should receive useful guidance, book the right appointment, complete intake before arriving, and get relevant follow-up afterward. The chosen service, appointment time, intake answers, visit notes, and follow-up status should appear together in one staff record.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="I turned operating knowledge into product rules" media>
  A model that could invent a treatment or ignore the spa's operating rules would make the recommendation unusable. Every answer had to come from an active service and follow the spa's rules for the guest's goal and available time.

<DecisionBlock title="Apply hard eligibility rules before AI ranking.">
  Airtable stores the service name, duration, goals, description, status, and booking group. Make
  removes inactive services and anything that doesn't fit the guest's available time. DeepSeek can
  rank the remaining options, but it can't introduce a treatment outside that shortlist.
</DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock title="Separate the customer conversation from the operating logic.">
  Typebot asks the questions and shows the result. Make applies the transformations, Airtable stores
  the editable catalog, and Cal.com remains the source of truth for available appointment times.
  This is easier to inspect and change than a large conversation canvas filled with service-specific branches.
</DecisionBlock>

  <DeviceGrid screens={ruleScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Guests can ask for help without leaving the spa website" media wideMedia>
  I placed the concierge inside a complete spa website so the prototype could be judged as a customer journey rather than a collection of automation tools. The site presents all 20 services, but the persistent concierge gives an uncertain visitor a simpler starting point.

The conversation combines a free-text request with goal, duration, and experience preferences. It returns one concise recommendation and offers booking, changing the answers, or asking the spa for help. Contact details are requested only after the guest chooses booking or staff help, keeping lead capture tied to intent.

  <DeviceGrid screens={guestScreens} stacked />
  <DeviceGrid screens={conciergeScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="One recommendation becomes a bookable appointment" media>
  Twenty services map into six shared booking groups instead of requiring a separate Cal.com event for every treatment. The concierge opens the matching booking group and passes the service name and duration in the URL. Cal.com remains the source of truth for available times and the appointment itself.

After booking, Tally receives the appointment context through hidden fields. The guest sees the details already on file and answers only the preparation questions that staff need.

  <DeviceGrid screens={bookingScreens} />
  <DeviceGrid screens={confirmationScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Staff need an operating surface, not another inbox" media>
  The Airtable interface gives staff a daily visit queue, pending-intake view, appointments needing attention, and an editable service catalog. Staff can review the visit, record notes, and mark it complete from the same workspace.

Completing the visit starts the follow-up flow. Make sends approved aftercare guidance and requests feedback, while the appointment record shows what has already happened and what still needs attention.

  <DeviceGrid screens={staffScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Every handoff happens automatically" media>
  I split the automation into named business events rather than one large scenario. A booking creates the appointment and preparation email. Intake updates the visit record. Visit completion sends aftercare and feedback. Submitted feedback returns to Airtable and can alert the owner when follow-up is requested.

The same pattern supports the concierge. Typebot sends the guest's answers to Make, Make loads the eligible Airtable records, and the structured recommendation returns to the conversation.

  <DeviceGrid screens={automationScreens} />
  <DeviceGrid screens={followUpScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>
