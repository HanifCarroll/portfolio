<CaseSection title="A long service menu left guests unsure what to book">
  I built Tranquility as an independent prototype for a fictional spa after an operations consultant described a familiar customer problem. A visitor reaches a long service menu and can't tell which treatment fits. They leave the decision for later.

The prototype had to do more than answer a question. It needed to carry the chosen treatment into the appointment. Staff would then see the guest's intake in the same Airtable record and use it to close the visit.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="I turned operating knowledge into product rules" media>
  A model that could invent a treatment or ignore the spa's operating rules would make the recommendation unusable. Every answer had to come from an active service and follow the spa's rules for the guest's goal and available time.

<DecisionBlock title="Apply hard eligibility rules before AI ranking.">
  Each Airtable record defines whether a service can be recommended and where the guest can book it.
  Make removes anything that doesn't fit the selected duration. DeepSeek ranks only the records that remain.
</DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock title="Separate the customer conversation from the operating logic.">
  Typebot runs the questions and displays the result. Make reads the catalog from Airtable and returns
  an eligible treatment. Cal.com supplies the available times. Changing the conversation doesn't require
  editing the booking logic.
</DecisionBlock>

  <DeviceGrid screens={ruleScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Guests can ask for help without leaving the spa website" media wideMedia>
  I placed the concierge inside a complete spa website so the prototype could be judged as a customer journey rather than a collection of automation tools. The site presents all 20 services, but the persistent concierge gives an uncertain visitor a simpler starting point.

Visitors describe what they want and how long they have. Typebot may ask what kind of experience they prefer before returning a service. Contact details appear only when someone books or asks staff for help.

  <DeviceGrid screens={guestScreens} stacked />
  <DeviceGrid screens={conciergeScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="One recommendation becomes a bookable appointment" media>
  Twenty services map into six shared booking groups instead of requiring a separate Cal.com event for every treatment. The concierge opens the matching booking group and passes the service name and duration in the URL. Cal.com supplies the live times and stores the appointment.

After booking, Tally receives the appointment context through hidden fields. The guest sees the details already on file and answers only the preparation questions that staff need.

  <DeviceGrid screens={bookingScreens} />
  <DeviceGrid screens={confirmationScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Staff run the day from one Airtable queue" media>
  The Airtable interface opens on the day's visit queue. Separate views surface missing intake and other appointments that need attention. Staff work from the appointment record before and after the visit, while the service catalog remains available in the same interface.

When staff mark the visit complete, Make sends the recorded aftercare and opens the feedback step. Airtable shows whether either action still needs attention.

  <DeviceGrid screens={staffScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Each business event has its own Make scenario" media>
  Each scenario begins with a specific event. A new booking creates the Airtable appointment and sends the preparation email. Intake updates that record. When staff close the visit, Make sends the recorded aftercare and requests feedback. If the guest asks to be contacted, the owner receives an alert.

The concierge uses the same boundary. Typebot sends the answers to Make and receives a structured recommendation built from the eligible Airtable records.

  <DeviceGrid screens={automationScreens} />
  <DeviceGrid screens={followUpScreens} stacked />
</CaseSection>
