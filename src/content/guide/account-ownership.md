## What it is

Every tool your business uses has an owner account: the person or email address that can change the password, pay the bill, add or remove users, and close the account. If a designer, developer, agency, or employee set up your domain, website, or social accounts under their own email, they may control access, even if you paid. Who legally owns a domain or website can depend on who is listed as the registrant and on your contract, so check the registrar record, each service's user roles, and the contract separately. The billing contact, the administrator, and the legal owner aren't always the same person.

## When you need it

- You don't know who registered your domain or which email the website account uses.
- A former employee or designer still controls an account you need.
- Bills for your tools go to someone else's card or email.
- Someone left, and you can't get into an account they set up.

## Popular choices

- **A business email for accounts**: Sign up for business tools with a business address you control, such as admin@yourbusiness.com, not a staff member's personal email. For your domain registrar, also add a recovery email or phone number that doesn't depend on your domain, because if the domain expires, you can't receive email at it.
- **A password manager**: Store the owner login for each account in a vault the business controls, and let only the owner and one chosen backup person open it. Staff get their own logins for daily work. See the password managers entry.
- **A simple account list**: A sheet with each tool, its owner email, who has access, the renewal date, and which card pays for it, named by its label or last four digits. Don't write full card numbers in the sheet.

## What to watch for

- **Domain first.** Whoever owns your domain decides where your website and email point, so check its owner before anything else. If you don't know where it's registered, search your email for a renewal receipt. For .com and similar endings, ICANN's lookup tool usually shows the registrar, though often not the owner's details. For .ar domains, use the search on NIC Argentina's site.
- **Admin, not owner.** Some services let you add others as admins while you stay the owner. Give contractors admin or editor access, not ownership.
- **When someone leaves.** Before you remove anything, sign in to each shared owner account in a private or incognito browser window, so your existing sign-in isn't reused, with a login and second step the business controls, and check that it works. Then turn off their access on the day they leave. If they used a shared owner login, change its password, sign them out of active sessions where the service allows it, and remove their phone, email, and devices from the account's recovery and sign-in options. Move their business files and records to the business before you delete their own named account. For daily work, give each person their own named login instead of sharing the owner login.
