## What it is

Every tool your business uses has an owner account: the person or email address that can change the password, pay the bill, add or remove users, and close the account. If a designer, developer, agency, or employee set up your domain, website, or social accounts under their own email, they own them, even if you paid.

## When you need it

- You don't know who registered your domain or which email the website account uses.
- A former employee or designer still controls an account you need.
- Bills for your tools go to someone else's card or email.
- Someone left, and you can't get into an account they set up.

## Popular choices

- **A business email for accounts**: Sign up for business tools with a business address you control, such as admin@yourbusiness.com, not a staff member's personal email.
- **A password manager**: Store the owner login for each account in a shared vault the business controls. See the password managers entry.
- **A simple account list**: A sheet with each tool, its owner email, who has access, the renewal date, and the payment card.

## What to watch for

- **Domain first.** Whoever owns your domain decides where your website and email point, so check its owner before anything else.
- **Admin, not owner.** Some services let you add others as admins while you stay the owner. Give contractors admin or editor access, not ownership.
- **When someone leaves.** Remove their access and change shared passwords on the day they leave.
