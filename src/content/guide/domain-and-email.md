## What it is

A domain is your web address, such as yourbusiness.com. You rent it by the year from a company called a registrar. Business email means email addresses that end in your domain, such as hola@yourbusiness.com, from a service that stores and sends that email.

## When you need it

- You use a Gmail or Hotmail address for the business, and customers can't tell it's really you.
- You don't know who registered your website's domain, or which account it's in.
- Several people answer customer email, and you want addresses such as ventas@ or reservas@ that stay with the business when people leave.

## Popular choices

- **Cloudflare Registrar**: A registrar for many common endings, such as .com. Cloudflare says it charges what registries and ICANN charge it, with no markup. A .com costs $11.17 a year to register or renew.
- **Namecheap**: A registrar with a long list of domain endings. The listed renewal price for a .com is $19.98 a year. Namecheap notes that ICANN's $0.20 yearly fee is added at checkout for some domains.
- **NIC Argentina**: The official registry for domains that end in .ar, such as .com.ar. You register a domain for one year and renew it each year, and NIC Argentina lists the price on its site.
- **Google Workspace**: Business email in Gmail, plus Calendar and Drive, on your own domain. Business Starter costs $7 per user a month on a yearly plan, or $8.40 on a flexible monthly plan.
- **Microsoft 365**: Business email in Outlook, plus Word, Excel, and OneDrive, on your own domain. Business Basic costs $7 per user a month paid yearly, or $8.40 with a monthly subscription.

Prices as of November 2026.

## What to watch for

- **Whose name is on the domain.** Register the domain in an account the business controls, with a business email address. Also add a recovery email or phone number that doesn't depend on the domain, because if the domain expires, you can't receive email at it. If a designer or former employee registered it, ask them to move it to you. If you don't know where it's registered, search your email for a renewal receipt. For .com and similar endings, ICANN's lookup tool usually shows the registrar, though often not the owner's details. For .ar domains, use the search on NIC Argentina's site.
- **Renewal dates.** If you miss a renewal, someone else can register your domain. Turn on automatic renewal and keep the payment card up to date.
- **Two separate bills.** The domain and the email service are often from different companies, so you pay two renewals.
- **Moving email later.** You can move email to another service, but plan it so no messages go missing during the move.
