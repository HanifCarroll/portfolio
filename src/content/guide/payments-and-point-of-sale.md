## What it is

A payment provider is a company that charges a customer's card or wallet and sends the money to your bank account, minus a fee. A point-of-sale app, or POS, is the app at your counter that records each sale. Many providers sell both: a small card reader, an app on a phone or tablet, and payment links you can send by message.

## When you need it

- Customers ask to pay by card or phone and you can only take cash or a bank transfer.
- You send your bank details by message and then check your account to see who paid.
- You add up the day's sales by hand at closing time.
- You want to know which products or services sell best and you can't tell from your notes.

## Popular choices

- **Mercado Pago**: Widely used in Argentina and other Latin American countries, with card readers, QR codes, and payment links. In Argentina, the fee on a Point card reader is 2.99% for debit and 5.99% for credit when you get the money right away, and lower if you wait. With a QR code, the fee starts at 0.80% when the customer pays from their Mercado Pago balance. A payment link costs 6.29% when you get the money right away and 1.49% when you wait 35 days. These fees don't include IVA, and payment link costs can vary by province because of provincial taxes.
- **Square**: A card reader and a POS app. It fits a café, shop, or stall in the countries where Square operates. In the U.S., the Square Free plan costs $0 a month per location, and card fees are 2.6% + 15¢ in person and 3.3% + 30¢ online. Your first magstripe reader is free, and the contactless and chip reader costs $59.
- **SumUp**: Small card readers for businesses that take a few card payments a day. In the U.S., the fee is 2.6% + 10¢ per in-person payment, and the Plus reader lists at $54. Tap to Pay on your phone has no reader cost.
- **Stripe**: Payment links and online checkout. It fits you if most of your sales happen online. In the U.S., the fee is 2.9% + 30¢ per successful payment with a domestic card, and payment links have no extra fee.

Prices as of November 2026.

## What to watch for

- **The fee on each payment.** Providers take a percentage of each sale, and the rate often changes by card type, by country, and by how soon you want the money.
- **When the money arrives.** Some providers hold money for days before it reaches your bank, or charge more for faster transfers.
- **Hardware you can't reuse.** A card reader usually works only with its own provider.
- **Exporting your sales.** Check that you can download sales as a spreadsheet for your accountant.
