## What it is

"The cloud" means computers in a company's data centers that you use over the internet. When you save a file to Google Drive, use an online booking app, or check your bank account in a browser, the software and your information are stored on that company's computers. Copies can also stay on your own devices when you download or sync files.

## When you need it

- You want to open your files and apps from any device, not one computer in the shop.
- You don't want to buy, update, and repair your own server.
- Several people need to see the same information at the same time.

## Popular choices

- **Cloud apps you already use**: Gmail, Google Drive, Microsoft 365, WhatsApp backups, and most booking and payment apps run in the cloud.
- **Cloud storage**: Google Drive, OneDrive, and Dropbox store your files.
- **Cloud hosting**: Companies such as Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and Cloudflare rent computers for websites and custom software. You usually reach them through a developer or a website builder.

## What to watch for

- **Internet access.** Cloud apps need a connection. Check whether your most important apps work offline, such as a POS that records sales without internet.
- **Someone else's computers.** The company stores your information, so read where it's stored, who can see it, and how to export it.
- **Monthly costs.** Cloud services usually charge every month or year instead of once.
