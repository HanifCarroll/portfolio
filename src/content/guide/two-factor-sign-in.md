## What it is

Two-factor sign-in, also called two-factor authentication or 2FA, asks for a second step after your password. The second step is usually a code from an app on your phone, a code sent by text message, a tap on a prompt on your phone, or a passkey. A passkey is a sign-in that your phone or computer stores, which you unlock with your fingerprint, face, or screen lock. With two-factor sign-in, someone who steals your password still can't sign in.

## When you need it

- You haven't turned it on for your email, bank, payment provider, domain registrar, or social accounts.
- You've had an account taken over, or a customer told you about a strange message from your account.
- Several people share one login, and you can't tell who signed in.

Start with your email account, because anyone who controls your email can reset most of your other passwords.

## Popular choices

- **An authenticator app**: Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, or a password manager that stores codes.
- **Passkeys**: Supported by Google, Apple, Microsoft, and a growing number of other services.
- **Text message codes**: Better than a password alone, but weaker than an app or passkey, because someone can move your phone number to their SIM card.

## What to watch for

- **Backup codes.** Save the backup codes each service gives you when you turn on two-factor sign-in, and store them somewhere safe. Without them, a lost phone can lock you out.
- **Shared accounts.** If staff share an account, give each person their own login where the service allows it, so each has their own second step.
- **Phone numbers you might lose.** Don't use a staff member's personal phone as the only second step on a business account.
